---
name: Reference fidelity
description: How to treat the user's approved visual references for Tanda.
---
When the user says to match the Tanda picture exactly, reproduce its composition, density, rounded typography, coral accents, illustration and card treatment rather than inventing another direction.

**Why:** The user repeatedly rejected loose editorial reinterpretations and replacement artwork, saying “do exactly as in the picture.”

**How to apply:** Use user-supplied replacement artwork directly, without tracing or redrawing it, and keep semantic text and working controls separate. The latest explicit reference takes precedence over earlier stylistic choices.

Judge fidelity by the whole composition at the reference's viewport, including relative scale, overlap and vertical density—not merely whether the same elements are present.

**Why:** The user repeated “exactly like this” after rejecting versions whose phones, illustration and section spacing still differed substantially.

**How to apply:** Compare the complete page at the reference dimensions before calling a visual match finished, while also checking the responsive mobile layout.

Preserve the reference's relative scale on wider desktop windows, not just at the reference image's width.

**Why:** The user rejected a comparison where capped content widths, type and mockups made the real page look miniature despite a closer narrow-screen rendering.

**How to apply:** Compare both the reference-width composition and a wider desktop viewport. Scale related typography, mockups, artwork and section spacing together rather than independently capping them. Constrain the whole composition by available height as well as width so wide, short windows do not become oversized.

The phone mockups must leave the woman/cyclist and the background church visible.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected oversized phones covering those parts of the illustration, then rejected floating phones with empty space underneath.

**How to apply:** Preserve clear space around those foreground characters and landmarks, while grounding the phone pair near the illustrated baseline instead of leaving a large empty area below it.

Keep subject isolation separate from background blending when compositing the city artwork.

**Why:** The raster layer sources contain complete scenes rather than isolated objects; widening a background blend can expose duplicate actors or vehicle parts.

**How to apply:** Inspect joins for both seams and duplicated subjects. Soften background transitions without expanding visible foreground content indiscriminately.

Treat screenshot crops as references, not final production artwork. Use deliberately authored vector artwork or a genuinely high-resolution original before adding motion. Do not auto-trace the screenshot.

**Why:** The user rejected the screenshot-derived hero as blurry, then rejected automatic tracing because the faces, lettering and shapes were distorted. They explicitly asked to fix artwork before animation.

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved composition without stretching or tracing screenshots. Inspect faces, hands, lettering and linework close-up before claiming quality is fixed; sharp edges alone are not enough.

Without layered source art, prefer localized motion to translating complex foreground actors across the scene.

**Why:** Whole-vehicle cutouts require reconstructing hidden backgrounds, which previously degraded the approved artwork. Limited cabin motion and wheel details preserve the people, architecture and vehicle bodies.

**How to apply:** Keep the uploaded source unchanged, isolate only small moving details, and verify their motion and pause/reduced-motion behavior without introducing broad background patches.
